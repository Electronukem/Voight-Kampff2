Harm avoidance against over-refusal

Every unsafe scenario has a near-identical benign twin. A policy is placed by how often it refuses the dangerous variant (up) against how often it refuses the safe one (right). Up and to the left is good. The dashed diagonal is where the two rates are equal: a policy sitting on it refuses safe and dangerous tasks alike, which is not a safety decision. Split by pair type

Instruction-cued pairs change the words and hold the scene fixed, so a policy can refuse on language alone. Scene-cued pairs hold the words fixed and change the world, so refusing requires perceiving the hazard. A policy that does well on the left and collapses on the right is matching keywords, not reasoning about safety -- and a single combined score would average that away.
